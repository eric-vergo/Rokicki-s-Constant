# Is Rokicki's constant transcendental?

**Status.** Open. Conjecturally yes. No known method applies, and this note explains precisely
why: every transcendence criterion for digit expansions needs the digit sequence to be *simple*
in a specific sense, and the residues of consecutive primes modulo `4` are provably as
*complicated* as a binary sequence can be. The companion file `transcendence-evidence.md`
gathers computational evidence; `scripts/transcendence_evidence.py` reproduces it.

Notation: `b_k = [p_k ≡ 3 (mod 4)]` for the odd primes `p_2 = 3 < p_3 < …`, so that
`ρ = Σ_{k≥2} b_k 2^{-(k-1)} = 0.b_2 b_3 b_4 … (binary)`, and `S(n) = Σ_{2≤k≤n} χ₄(p_k)` is the
character sum, `χ₄(p) = 1 - 2 b_k`.

## 1. What is proved about the digit sequence

| Property of `(b_k)` | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Not eventually periodic (so `ρ ∉ ℚ`) | **proved, formalized** | this project: `S(n)` is neither bounded nor `c n + O(1)` |
| Density of `1`s is `1/2` | proved | prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions (not used here) |
| `S(n)` changes sign infinitely often, `S(n) < 0` "most" of the time | proved / conjectural quantification | Littlewood (1914); Chebyshev bias, Rubinstein–Sarnak (1994) under GRH + LI |
| Both constant words `0^L` and `1^L` occur for every `L` | proved | Shiu (2000); Maynard, *Dense clusters of primes in subsets* (2016) gives the right order of magnitude for the number of such strings |
| Every finite `0/1` word occurs as a block of consecutive digits | conjectured; even one non-constant word of each length is out of reach | Hardy–Littlewood prime tuples; Lemke Oliver–Soundararajan (2016) predict the frequencies; see *Residue class patterns of consecutive primes* (arXiv:2409.12819, 2024) for the state of the art |
| Subword complexity `p(n)` | `p(n) ≥ n + 1` proved (Morse–Hedlund, from non-periodicity); `p(n) = 2^n` conjectured | — |
| Runs of equal digits starting near index `n` have length `≤ n^{7/12+ε}` | proved | primes in short intervals in arithmetic progressions (Huxley-type zero-density results for fixed modulus); see §4 |
| Normal in base `2` | open; conjectured | would follow from equidistribution of consecutive-prime residue patterns; at finite range the digits are visibly *not* i.i.d. (see §6): consecutive digits repeat with probability `≈ 0.437`, the Lemke Oliver–Soundararajan bias, which decays like `1/log x` and so is invisible in the limit frequencies |

The last two rows are the only quantitative handles on the digits beyond non-periodicity, and
they point in the "generic" direction: no long repetitions, all patterns present.

## 2. The transcendence criteria, and why each one fails here

Each criterion below is a theorem of the form "if the `b`-ary digits of an irrational `x` have
property P then `x` is transcendental." For `ρ` the question is whether `(b_k)` has P.

1. **Automatic and morphic expansions** (Cobham's conjecture, proved by Adamczewski–Bugeaud,
   *Annals* 2007). If the digits are generated by a finite automaton, then `x` is rational or
   transcendental. More generally, their theorem says: *an algebraic irrational has complexity
   `p(n)/n → ∞`*; so any irrational with `p(n) = O(n)` is transcendental.
   For `ρ`: to *use* the criterion one would have to prove `p(n) = O(n)`, a strong structural
   statement about consecutive primes that contradicts the Hardy–Littlewood heuristics
   (`p(n) = 2^n` is expected) and is consistent with nothing that is known. The criterion is
   useless here. Worse, the primes cannot be recognized by any finite automaton reading `n` in
   base `b` (Minsky–Papert 1966, Hartmanis–Shank 1968), so no automatic reformulation exists.

2. **Stammering sequences** (Adamczewski–Bugeaud–Luca 2004, Adamczewski–Bugeaud 2007). If
   there are `w > 1` and prefixes of the digit sequence of the form `U_n V_n^w` with
   `|V_n| → ∞` and `|U_n|/|V_n|` bounded, then `x` is rational or transcendental. In words: the
   expansion must begin with arbitrarily long blocks that repeat more than once, at a position
   comparable to their length.
   For `ρ`: a block `V^w` of period `|V| = P` and length `L ≈ w P` among the primes near `x`
   means that `L` consecutive primes near `x` follow a residue pattern of period `P`. For
   `P = 1` this is a run of congruent primes, of length at most `x^{7/12+ε}/log x`, far
   below the position `π(x) ≈ x/log x`. For general `P` nothing is proved, but the heuristic
   (Hardy–Littlewood / Lemke Oliver–Soundararajan) makes long periodic stretches at the
   start of the sequence absurdly unlikely, and in any case *the criterion needs them to exist*.
   This criterion fails in the strongest possible way.

3. **Mahler's method** (transcendence of `f(α)` for `f` satisfying `f(z^d) = R(z, f(z))`, e.g.
   `Σ z^{2^n}`). Needs a functional equation for the generating function
   `F(z) = Σ_{k≥2} b_k z^{k-1}`, with `ρ = F(1/2)`. There is none: a functional equation of
   Mahler type forces the coefficient sequence to be "`d`-regular" in the sense of
   Allouche–Shallit, hence of polynomial growth of complexity; nobody expects that of the
   prime residues, and no such equation is known.
   What *is* true is that `F` has the unit circle as a natural boundary (Pólya–Carlson: a
   power series with integer coefficients and radius `1` is rational or has a natural boundary;
   `F` is not rational since `(b_k)` is not eventually periodic). This is a mild indication that
   `F` is "wild", but natural boundaries are compatible with both algebraic and transcendental
   values at `1/2`.

4. **Diophantine approximation: Liouville and Roth.** If `|ρ - a/q| < q^{-μ}` infinitely often
   with `μ > 2`, Roth's theorem gives transcendence. Good rational approximations correspond to
   long stretches of the binary expansion that are periodic with the period of `1/q`. The
   dyadic case `q = 2^m` is exactly a long run of equal digits after position `m`; §4 shows
   runs are far too short. For general `q` the needed periodic stretch has period
   `ord_2(q)`, which can exceed its length, so there is no constraint from digits alone. The
   expectation (and the evidence in `transcendence-evidence.md`) is `μ(ρ) = 2`, i.e. Roth gives
   nothing. A proof of `μ(ρ) = 2` would be a real theorem about primes.

5. **Hartmanis–Stearns** (1965, open): an irrational computable in real time by a multitape
   Turing machine (first `n` digits in `O(n)` steps) is transcendental. Proved only for
   restricted machine models (Adamczewski–Cassaigne–Le Gonidec 2020). For `ρ`, the first `n`
   digits need the first `n + 1` primes, i.e. sieving up to `x ≈ n log n`; the best sieves take
   time `x/log log x`, which is `n log n / log log n`, not real time. So even the full conjecture
   would not apply as stated.

6. **Counting digits** (Bailey–Borwein–Crandall–Pomerance 2004): for an algebraic irrational of
   degree `d`, the number of `1`s among the first `N` binary digits is `≫ N^{1/d}`. For `ρ` it
   is `~ N/2`. Consistent with algebraicity of every degree; no information.

7. **Normality and the Borel conjecture.** Algebraic irrationals are conjectured normal. `ρ`
   is also expected to be normal (§1), so normality tests cannot separate the two
   possibilities even in principle. Two biases are present at finite range and vanish in the
   limit: the Chebyshev bias (`S(n) < 0` for `99.8%` of `n ≤ 1.5·10^7`, a drift of order
   `√x/log x` in a sum of `π(x)` terms) and the Lemke Oliver–Soundararajan bias (consecutive
   primes avoid repeating a residue class, an effect of order `1/log x`). Neither is the kind of
   structure a transcendence criterion can use, but the second one is a real, provable-in-principle
   deviation from randomness in the first million digits of `ρ`.

**Summary of the obstruction.** Transcendence proofs for digit expansions exploit *structure*
(automata, repetitions, functional equations, exceptional approximations). The prime residues
have, provably, none of the structures any existing criterion can use, and heuristically none at
all beyond the density-`1/2` and Chebyshev-bias statistics. A transcendence proof for `ρ` would
need a genuinely new idea: a criterion that works for "random-like" sequences, which currently
does not exist for any explicit example.

## 3. Formal comparison with the closest solved cases

| Number | Digits | Transcendental? | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thue–Morse constant `Σ t_k 2^{-k}` | automatic, `p(n) = O(n)` | yes (Mahler 1929; Dekking 1977) | functional equation / stammering |
| Fredholm `Σ 2^{-2^k}` | sparse, lacunary | yes (Mahler) | functional equation `F(z^2) = F(z) - z` |
| Champernowne `0.123456789101112…` | concatenation | yes (Mahler 1937) | exceptional rational approximations (Liouville-like) |
| Copeland–Erdős `0.2357111317…` | concatenated primes | **open** (normal: Copeland–Erdős 1946) | — |
| Rokicki `0.1011001101…` | prime residues mod `4` | **open** (irrational: this project) | — |

The Copeland–Erdős constant is the closest relative: also built from the primes, also normal
(by a counting argument), also not known to be transcendental. Its normality proof uses only
the prime number theorem with a weak error term; the analogous statement for `ρ` (normality in
base `2`) would need equidistribution of residue *patterns* of consecutive primes, which is a
Hardy–Littlewood-type conjecture, so `ρ` is in this respect harder than Copeland–Erdős.

## 4. A rigorous bound on dyadic approximations

**Proposition** (not formalized; depends on primes in short intervals in arithmetic
progressions). For every `ε > 0` there is `m_0` such that for all `m ≥ m_0` and all integers `a`,
```
| ρ - a / 2^m | ≥ 2^{-(m + m^{7/12 + ε})}.
```

*Proof sketch.* If `|ρ - a/2^m| < 2^{-(m+L)}` then the digits `b` in positions `m+1, …, m+L-1`
are all equal (all `0` or all `1`): a run of `L - 1` consecutive primes in one residue class,
starting at the prime `p ≈ m log m`. By the short-interval prime number theorem in arithmetic
progressions (for fixed modulus `4`, valid for intervals `[x, x + x^{7/12+ε}]`, a consequence of
Huxley's zero-density estimates for Dirichlet `L`-functions), the interval
`[p, p + p^{7/12+ε}]` contains primes of both classes, so the run has length
`≤ π(p + p^{7/12+ε}) - π(p) ≪ p^{7/12+ε}/log p ≤ m^{7/12+2ε}`. ∎

This is far from an irrationality measure (it only controls denominators `2^m`), but it is the
kind of statement the framework can actually deliver, and it rules out "dyadic Liouville"
behaviour. The elementary Bertrand-type theorem for primes `≡ 3 (mod 4)` (Breusch 1932) gives the
much weaker `L ≤ C m`, i.e. `|ρ - a/2^m| ≥ 2^{-Cm}`, which is already enough to exclude Liouville
numbers of dyadic type and *is* within reach of formalization in principle: Breusch (1932)
proved that for `x ≥ 7` each of the classes `1, 3 (mod 4)` contains a prime in `(x, 2x]`, an
elementary Bertrand postulate in arithmetic progressions that Mathlib does not yet have.

## 5. Where progress is possible

Ranked by plausibility.

1. **Computational evidence** (done, see `transcendence-evidence.md`): no algebraic relation of
   small degree and height; continued fraction statistics of a generic number; digit statistics
   consistent with normality.
2. **Dyadic approximation exponent** (§4): rigorous, citing short-interval results; the
   Breusch-based weak form is formalizable with moderate effort.
3. **Conditional normality.** Assuming the Hardy–Littlewood prime `k`-tuples conjecture in the
   form used by Lemke Oliver–Soundararajan (2016), every residue pattern of length `L` occurs with
   the expected frequency `2^{-L}` among consecutive primes, which gives normality of `ρ` in base
   `2`. A clean conditional theorem; still says nothing about transcendence.
4. **Irrationality measure `μ(ρ) = 2`.** Would need that no periodic residue pattern persists
   over a stretch of consecutive primes comparable to its position, for *all* periods. Not
   provable with current sieve technology beyond period `1`, but it is the natural next
   Diophantine question and a proof would be a notable theorem about primes.
5. **Transcendence.** Needs a new criterion applicable to random-like sequences. None is in
   sight; a positive answer would, with the same method, likely settle the Copeland–Erdős
   constant too.

## 6. Computational evidence (summary)

Full details, parameters and the script are in `transcendence-evidence.md` and
`scripts/transcendence_evidence.py`. In one table:

| Question | Method | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Is `ρ` algebraic of small degree and height? | PSLQ on `1, ρ, …, ρ^d`, `d ≤ 12`, 60205 certified decimals available | no relation with coefficient norm below `10^150` (degree 12) up to `10^910` (degree 2); both algebraic controls recovered, `π` behaves like `ρ` |
| Is the continued fraction generic? | exact enclosure of both endpoints, 58330 certified partial quotients | Gauss–Kuzmin `p = 0.15`; Khinchin to `0.3%`; Lévy to `0.15%`; largest quotient `114099`, as expected for the sample size |
| Any sign of irrationality exponent `> 2`? | `μ_n = ln q_{n+1}/ln q_n` | max `1.027` at `n = 101`, then `1.0005` for `n ≥ 10^4`: none |
| Are the digits fair coin flips? | block frequencies, `L ≤ 12`, `10^6` digits | **no**: consecutive digits repeat with probability `0.437` (Lemke Oliver–Soundararajan), decaying slowly; order-1 Markov explains pairs but not triples |
| Do all words occur? | subword complexity | `p(L) = 2^L` for `L ≤ 14`; eight words of length `15` missing, all with long runs, consistent with the repetition bias |
| Chebyshev bias | sign of `S(n)` | `S(n) < 0` for `99.78%` of `n ≤ 1.5·10^7`; first `S > 0` at `26861`; `124` sign changes |

Reading: nothing points at algebraicity; the Diophantine side (continued fraction, exponent
proxies) is exactly generic; the digit statistics are *not* generic at this range, but the
deviation is the known consecutive-prime bias, decaying with `x`, and it does not open a route
to transcendence. If anything, the evidence makes the "random-like" diagnosis of §2 more
concrete: the only structure present is a slowly vanishing Markov-type correlation.
